17 世紀之後｜公司、殖民與工業化

主線停在 17 世紀荷蘭人來到臺灣。這一頁往下走兩百年：一家公司為什麼能築堡、統治臺灣？一杯茶怎麼牽動鴉片和白銀？機器又怎麼一台帶出下一台？

四段故事

一、會築堡的公司

1662 年鄭成功攻下熱蘭遮城。當時在臺灣的荷蘭人，其實是一家公司的人員：荷蘭東印度公司，簡稱 VOC。

17 世紀初，歐洲人搶著去亞洲買香料。英國在 1600 年由女王特許成立英國東印度公司；荷蘭許多小公司則互相競爭，荷蘭國會在 1602 年把它們合併成一家 VOC。

國會給 VOC 的特許權很大：在亞洲可以築堡、僱用士兵、和當地統治者締約，必要時還能作戰。這些通常是國家才做的事，政府交給公司，換來貿易利潤。

錢從哪裡來？VOC 向大眾募股：有錢人多買，小商人少買，股份還能轉手賣給別人。阿姆斯特丹因此出現熱絡的股份買賣，是今天股票市場的前身之一。

為了獨占肉豆蔻，VOC 在 1621 年以武力征服班達群島，之後由公司控制香料的生產與買賣。

兩家東印度公司比一比

一句話記：荷蘭看東南亞和臺灣，英國看印度和中國的茶。

二、公司在臺灣的三十八年

17 世紀的臺灣西部平原，到處是成群的梅花鹿。

1624 年，荷蘭人在大員（今臺南安平）築熱蘭遮城。臺灣剛好位在中國、日本、東南亞航線的中間，中國的生絲、瓷器可以在這裡轉運，是做轉口貿易的好地點。

公司很快發現臺灣的寶物：鹿皮。原住民打獵，漢人商人收購，公司把鹿皮大量運到日本。幾十年下來，平原上的鹿群大大減少。

公司也招募福建的漢人來臺開墾，種稻和甘蔗，並向漢人收人頭稅。1652 年發生郭懷一事件，漢人農民起事，被荷蘭人鎮壓。

1661 年鄭成功率軍渡海，包圍熱蘭遮城約九個月；1662 年初，荷蘭長官揆一投降，荷蘭人離開臺灣。

三、為了一杯茶

18 世紀，英國人愛上了喝茶。茶幾乎只能向清朝買，而清朝不太需要英國的貨物，英國只好付白銀，白銀大量流進中國。

英國人找到一樣能把白銀換回來的商品：鴉片。英國東印度公司在統治的孟加拉實施鴉片專賣：鴉片在加爾各答拍賣給私人商人，再由他們運到中國沿海。清朝禁止鴉片，所以這是走私。

賣鴉片得到的白銀，再拿去廣州買茶。鴉片越賣越多，白銀反過來流出中國。

1839 年林則徐在虎門銷毀鴉片；1840 年第一次鴉片戰爭爆發，1842 年簽訂南京條約，清朝割讓香港、開放五個通商口岸。

在印度，1857 年爆發大規模反抗；1858 年英國政府收回統治權，公司從此不再管理印度。荷蘭的 VOC 則在 1799 年底解散，殖民地交給荷蘭國家。

四、機器的連鎖反應

印度棉布又輕又鮮豔，在英國大受歡迎。英國商人想自己做棉布，可是紡紗、織布全靠一雙手。

1733 年的飛梭讓織布變快，結果紗不夠用。於是 1760 到 1770 年代接連出現珍妮紡紗機、水力紡紗機、走錠紡紗機，紡紗也變快了。機器越來越大，工人集中到工廠上班。

機器需要動力。1712 年左右，紐科門做出抽礦坑積水的蒸汽抽水機；1769 年瓦特以「分離冷凝器」取得專利，後來改良成能帶動機器旋轉的蒸汽機，工廠不必再蓋在河邊。產品多了要運輸，1830 年利物浦到曼徹斯特的鐵路通車。

英國有幾個有利條件：煤和鐵礦就近可挖、河運和海運方便、有資金投資工廠，也有海外市場。

工廠和礦坑裡有童工，城市因為人口快速增加而擁擠、衛生不良。1854 年倫敦霍亂，醫生斯諾追查到一口被汙染的水井，之後倫敦建了新的下水道。

法律也慢慢改變：1833 年工廠法禁止紡織廠雇用九歲以下兒童，並派督察檢查；1842 年礦業法禁止女性和十歲以下男孩下礦坑工作。

第 1 集The Company with Its Own Forts

這集在講什麼：1662 年鄭成功攻下熱蘭遮城時，在臺灣的荷蘭人其實是一家公司的人員。這集講英國和荷蘭東印度公司怎麼成立、特許狀給了它們哪些權力，以及 VOC 怎麼向大眾募股。

先認得這幾個字：charter 特許狀・shareholder 股東・monopoly 獨占・nutmeg 肉豆蔻・fort 堡壘

📄 English Script（跟著聽）

In 1662, Koxinga captured Fort Zeelandia in southern Taiwan, and the Dutch sailed away. Here is something many people don't know. The Dutch in Taiwan were not sent there by a king. They worked for a company.

Around 1600, Europeans were racing to Asia for spices like pepper, cloves and nutmeg. In England, the queen gave a group of merchants a royal charter, and the English East India Company was born in 1600. In the Netherlands, many small companies sailed to Asia at the same time, and they competed with each other. So in 1602, the Dutch parliament joined them into one: the Dutch East India Company, known by its Dutch letters, V O C.

A charter is a special permission from the government. The VOC's charter gave it powers that usually belong to a country. In Asia, it could build forts, hire soldiers, sign treaties with local rulers, and even fight wars. In return, the government hoped the company would bring wealth home to the Netherlands.

Sending ships to Asia cost a lot of money, and the trip was risky. Storms, disease and pirates could all ruin a voyage. So the VOC did something clever. It sold shares to the public. A rich merchant could buy many shares, and a shopkeeper could buy a few. If the ships came home full of spices, the shareholders shared the profit. If a ship was lost, the loss was shared too. Shares could also be sold to other people. In Amsterdam, buying and selling shares became a busy business, and it is one of the early roots of today's stock markets.

The VOC wanted full control of nutmeg, a spice that grew on the tiny Banda Islands in what is now Indonesia. In 1621, the company took the islands by force. After that, the company controlled how nutmeg was grown and sold.

The VOC built its Asian headquarters at Batavia, which is Jakarta today. From there, its ships traveled to India, China, Japan and, in 1624, to Taiwan. In the next episode, we follow those ships to Tayouan, the place we now call Anping.

第 2 集Thirty-Eight Years in Taiwan

這集在講什麼：荷蘭東印度公司在大員築熱蘭遮城，靠轉口貿易和鹿皮賺錢，也招漢人來開墾。1661 年鄭成功渡海圍城，1662 年揆一投降，荷蘭人離開臺灣。

先認得這幾個字：deer hide 鹿皮・settler 移民・tax 稅・siege 圍城・surrender 投降

📄 English Script（跟著聽）

Imagine the plains of western Taiwan four hundred years ago. Tall grass, rivers, and herds of sika deer moving together across the fields.

In 1624, the Dutch East India Company built Fort Zeelandia at Tayouan, which is Anping in Tainan today. Why here? Look at a map. Taiwan sits right between China, Japan and Southeast Asia. Chinese silk and porcelain could be brought to Taiwan and shipped on to Japan and other ports. For a trading company, it was a perfect stop in the middle of the sea.

Soon the company found another treasure: deer hides. Indigenous hunters caught the deer. Chinese traders bought the hides. The company shipped them to Japan, where leather was in high demand. After a few decades of hunting, the great herds were much smaller.

The company also wanted farms. It invited settlers from Fujian, across the strait, to grow rice and sugarcane. Sugar became another important product. The company collected a head tax from the Chinese settlers. In 1652, a farmer leader named Guo Huaiyi led an uprising, and the Dutch put it down.

Then came Koxinga. His army had been fighting the Qing on the coast of China, and he needed a new base. In 1661, he crossed the strait with a large fleet and surrounded Fort Zeelandia. The Dutch held out for about nine months. Early in 1662, the Dutch governor, Frederick Coyett, surrendered. The Dutch sailed away after thirty-eight years in Taiwan.

Koxinga died later that same year, but his family ruled Taiwan for about twenty more years. In 1683, the Qing Empire took control of the island.

Today you can still visit what is left of Fort Zeelandia in Anping. Its old brick walls remind us of a time when ships from Europe, China and Japan all met on this small island.

第 3 集All for a Cup of Tea

這集在講什麼：英國人愛喝茶，只能用白銀向清朝買。英國東印度公司在印度專賣鴉片，鴉片運到中國換回白銀，白銀從此倒流。之後有虎門銷煙、鴉片戰爭和南京條約；1858 年英國政府接管印度。

先認得這幾個字：silver 白銀・opium 鴉片・smuggle 走私・treaty 條約・port 港口

📄 English Script（跟著聽）

In the 1700s, the British fell in love with a drink: tea. Rich families drank it, then ordinary families drank it too. There was one problem. Almost all the tea came from China, and the Qing Empire did not want many British goods. So Britain paid in silver, and year after year, silver flowed into China.

At that time, foreign merchants were allowed to trade only at one port: Canton, which we now call Guangzhou. British ships sailed there, loaded chests of tea, and paid with silver.

British traders searched for something they could sell in China. They found opium. In Bengal, in India, the English East India Company now ruled. It controlled the opium trade there. The company sold the opium at auctions in Calcutta to private merchants. Those merchants carried it to the coast of China. The Qing government had banned opium, so this was smuggling.

Here is how the circle worked. Opium was sold in China for silver. The silver was used in Canton to buy tea for Britain. As more and more opium came in, the flow of silver turned around. Now silver was leaving China instead of arriving.

In 1839, the official Lin Zexu destroyed huge amounts of opium at Humen. In 1840, the First Opium War began. In 1842, the two sides signed the Treaty of Nanjing. China gave Hong Kong to Britain and opened five ports to trade: Guangzhou, Xiamen, Fuzhou, Ningbo and Shanghai.

The company's story was also changing in India. In 1857, a large rebellion spread across northern India. The next year, in 1858, the British government took over the rule of India, and the company no longer governed it.

And the Dutch company? It was dissolved at the end of 1799, and its lands passed to the Dutch state. Two giant companies, once as powerful as countries, both came to an end.

第 4 集The Machines That Chased Each Other

這集在講什麼：飛梭讓織布變快、紗就不夠，於是有了紡紗機；機器要動力，於是有了蒸汽機；產品要運送，於是有了鐵路。最後看城市的新問題，以及第一批保護童工的法律。

先認得這幾個字：bottleneck 瓶頸・spinning 紡紗・weaving 織布・steam engine 蒸汽機・child labor 童工

📄 English Script（跟著聽）

Cotton cloth from India was light, soft and full of color, and people in Britain loved it. British merchants wanted to make cotton cloth at home. But spinning thread and weaving cloth were both done by hand, and that was slow.

In 1733, John Kay patented the flying shuttle. It made weaving much faster. Wonderful, right? Not quite. Now the weavers used up thread faster than the spinners could make it. Thread became the bottleneck, the narrowest part of the pipe.

So inventors worked on the new problem. In the 1760s and 1770s came the spinning jenny, the water frame and the spinning mule. Now spinning was fast too. The machines were big and needed power, so workers no longer worked at home. They walked to a new kind of building: the factory.

Early factories sat beside rivers to use water wheels. But engines were changing. Around 1712, Thomas Newcomen had built a steam engine to pump water out of mines. In 1769, James Watt patented a separate condenser that saved a great deal of fuel, and later he improved his engine so it could turn machines. Factories no longer had to stay by the river.

Factories now made more goods than ever, and they needed to move them. In 1830, the Liverpool and Manchester Railway opened. Its steam trains, built by George and Robert Stephenson, carried people and goods between two busy cities.

Britain had several advantages. It had coal and iron close to the surface, good rivers and ports, people with money to invest, and markets overseas.

Factories also brought new problems. Children worked in mills and mines. Cities grew so fast that there were not enough clean water and drains. In 1854, cholera swept through London, and a doctor named John Snow traced the illness to one dirty water pump. Later, London built a new system of sewers.

Slowly, laws began to change. The Factory Act of 1833 banned children under nine from working in textile mills and sent inspectors to check. In 1842, a new law kept women and young boys out of the mines.

One invention led to the next, and step by step, the way people worked and lived was changed.
